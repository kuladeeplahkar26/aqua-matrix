Core Mathematical Modeling

Matrix Algebra Water Consumption Representation

In campus resource modeling, multi-variable telemetry is arranged into an $m \times n$ matrix $A \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$. Rows represent distinct campus geographic locations ($i = 1, \dots, m$), and columns represent discrete operational time intervals ($j = 1, \dots, n$). Each element $a_{ij}$ denotes the water volume consumed (in litres) at location $i$ during time slot $j$.

Campus Consumption Matrix $A$

Values $a_{ij}$ in Litres with row totals, column totals, and extremum indicators

Maximum Element ($\max a_{ij}$) Minimum Element ($\min a_{ij}$)
Matrix Dimensions: 5 × 4 ($m \times n$)
Maximum Consumption ($a_{max}$): 0 L
Minimum Consumption ($a_{min}$): 0 L

Row Totals (Location Aggregation)

Sum of consumption per campus facility
R_i = \sum_{j=1}^{n} a_{ij} \quad \text{for each location } i

Vector product formulation: $R = A \cdot \mathbf{1}_n$, where $\mathbf{1}_n$ is an $n \times 1$ column vector of ones. Each entry $R_i$ reveals the cumulative daily footprint of a specific department.

Column Totals (Time Slot Aggregation)

Sum of consumption per temporal window
C_j = \sum_{i=1}^{m} a_{ij} \quad \text{for each time slot } j

Vector product formulation: $C^T = \mathbf{1}_m^T \cdot A$, where $\mathbf{1}_m$ is an $m \times 1$ column vector of ones. This isolates campus-wide temporal surges across the daily cycle.

Total Campus Consumption

Matrix Frobenius Entrywise Sum
T = \sum_{i=1}^{m} \sum_{j=1}^{n} a_{ij} = \mathbf{1}_m^T \cdot A \cdot \mathbf{1}_n

The overall scalar sum equal to $\sum R_i = \sum C_j$. Represents the total volume demanded by the campus over the analyzed timeframe.

Arithmetic Means

Average per location and time interval
\bar{R}_i = \frac{R_i}{n}, \qquad \bar{C}_j = \frac{C_j}{m}

Normalized baseline metric evaluating whether a facility or temporal window exceeds typical central tendency.

Continuous & Discrete Calculus

Calculus Analysis: Rates of Change & Cumulative Integration

Calculus allows us to transition from static volume measurements to dynamic flow rates ($q(t) = \frac{dV}{dt}$) and cumulative volumetric integration ($V = \int q(t) dt$). In digital smart-campus networks where readings are recorded at discrete time nodes, we deploy finite difference approximations for derivatives and definite integrals for continuous pipeline models.

Theoretical Engineering Clarification: Discrete campus time logs provide numerical approximations of instantaneous rates (finite difference quotients $\frac{\Delta V}{\Delta t}$) rather than exact analytical derivatives $\frac{dV}{dt}$, and Riemann sums rather than exact integrals, unless continuous analytical flow models $q(t)$ are formally assumed.

Module A: Average Rate of Change

Secant line slope between discrete campus temporal states

Slope of Secant
\text{Average Rate of Change} = \frac{\Delta V}{\Delta t} = \frac{V_2 - V_1}{t_2 - t_1} \quad \left[\frac{\text{Litres}}{\text{Hour}}\right]

Module B: Finite Difference Analysis

Numerical approximation of the derivative $\frac{dV}{dt}$ across consecutive measurements

Derivative Approx
q_k \approx \frac{V_{k+1} - V_k}{\Delta t_k} \quad (\text{Forward Difference})

For consecutive two-hour time slots ($\Delta t = 2 \text{ hours}$), the estimated rate of consumption change quantifies whether demand is accelerating ($q' > 0$) or decelerating ($q' < 0$).

Time Slot Volume $V$ (L) $\Delta t$ (Hours) $\Delta V$ (L) Estimated Rate of Change Status

Module C: Continuous Integration & Cumulative Volume

Fundamental Theorem of Calculus applied to campus hydraulic flow rates

Definite Integral
V = \int_{a}^{b} q(t) \, dt

Continuous Flow Integrator

Default project benchmark: 6 L/min tap flow
Default project benchmark: 15 minutes

Mathematical Proof & Derivation

Step 1
Define the Rate Function:

Let $q(t)$ denote instantaneous volumetric flow rate $\left[\frac{\text{L}}{\text{min}}\right]$. For constant flow $q(t) = 6 \text{ L/min}$ over $t \in [0, 15]$:

V = \int_{0}^{15} 6 \, dt
Step 2
Antiderivative Evaluation:

By the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus, $\int k \, dt = k t + C$. Evaluating bounds:

V = [6t]_{0}^{15} = 6(15) - 6(0) = 90 \text{ Litres}
Step 3
Riemann Sum Connection:

In discrete campus data, cumulative volume is approximated by the Riemann sum: $V \approx \sum_{k=1}^{n} q(t_k^*) \Delta t_k$. As $\Delta t_k \to 0$, the sum converges to the definite integral.

AquaMatrix

Smart Campus Water Consumption Analysis and Optimization Using Matrix Algebra and Calculus

B.Tech First Year Project Engineering Mathematics Applied Linear Algebra Differential & Integral Calculus

Project Specifications

  • Project Title: AquaMatrix – Smart Campus Water Consumption Analysis and Optimization Using Matrix Algebra and Calculus
  • Project Type: Website-Based Digital Mathematical Model
  • Mathematics Used: Matrix Algebra (Vectors, 2D Arrays, Linear Combinations, Row/Column Sums) & Calculus (Secant Rates, Finite Difference Approximations, Definite Integration)
  • Technology Stack: HTML5, CSS3 (Modern Responsive Dashboard), Vanilla JavaScript (ES6+), Chart.js (Interactive Visualizations)
  • Architectural Paradigm: Pure client-side single page architecture with browser LocalStorage persistence (Zero backend, zero third-party telemetry, 100% offline executable)

Project Objective

The primary objective of AquaMatrix is to demonstrate how fundamental mathematical models taught in first-year engineering can organize, analyze, and interpret campus water consumption data to support informed, data-driven water conservation decisions.

Structure Multi-Variable Campus Data: Transform disparate physical water logs into rigorous mathematical matrix representations $A \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$.
Quantify Rates of Change: Apply calculus principles (secant lines and finite differences) to detect surge periods and anomalous spikes.
Integrate Cumulative Flow: Model physical pipelines via continuous rate integration $\int q(t) dt$ to bridge theoretical calculus with environmental engineering.

First-Year B.Tech Mathematics Formulation Summary

Concept Mathematical Expression Physical Interpretation in Smart Campus

Column Vector Total C_j = \sum_{i=1}^{m} a_{ij} = (\mathbf{1}_m^T \cdot A)_j Campus-wide water demand during temporal window $j$.

Definite Integral V = \int_{a}^{b} q(t) \, dt Total volume accumulated from continuous flow rate $q(t)$ over time interval $[a, b]$.
